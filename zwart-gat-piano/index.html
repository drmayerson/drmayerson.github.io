Method ("tap an object" mode)

  • Strike and tone: the recording is cut at the loudest moment. The tone f is the strongest spectral peak (60–6000 Hz) of the decay, with parabolic interpolation.
  • Decay: two independent estimates of the amplitude e-folding time τ. (1) Time domain: complex demodulation at f, zero-phase low-pass, straight-line fit of ln|envelope| from the peak down to −45 dB or 4× the noise floor. (2) Frequency domain: half-power width Δf of the peak in the unwindowed, zero-padded spectrum (Lorentzian: Q = f/Δf). We use (1) for Q ≥ 25, otherwise (2). Q ≡ π f τ, the same definition as for quasinormal modes (Q = ωR/2ωI).
  • Ring count: n = Q·ln(1000)/π ≈ 2.2 Q oscillations until the amplitude has dropped by 60 dB.
  • Black hole: the fundamental l = m = 2 quasinormal mode, Berti, Cardoso & Will (2006) fits: MωR = 1.5251 − 1.1568(1−χ)0.1292, Q = 0.7000 + 1.4187(1−χ)−0.4990. Q fixes the spin χ (Q is dimensionless, so it does not care about speed); then f fixes the mass M = MωR/(2π f) in units GM☉/c³ = 4.925 µs. Real speed, no time scaling: audio tones are where stellar-mass black holes ring.
  • Second tone ("hair" check): strongest other peak above 1.15 f, compared with the l = m = 3 mode of the same black hole (same fits, coefficients 1.8956, −1.3043, 0.1818).
  • Mass is in the detector frame (no redshift correction), which is why the GW150914 example comes out near 68 M☉ instead of the source-frame 63 M☉.

Method (piano mode)

  • Notes: the l = m = 2, 3, 4 fundamental (n = 0) Kerr QNMs from the Berti–Cardoso–Will fits (coefficients l = 3: 1.8956, −1.3043, 0.1818 / 0.9000, 2.3430, −0.4810; l = 4: 2.3000, −1.5056, 0.2244 / 1.1929, 3.1191, −0.4825). Mystery black holes: M log-uniform in 2.5–7.9 × 10⁶ M☉, χ uniform in 0–0.90. Free play: χ ≤ 0.95.
  • Response (toy, not the paper's calculation): each mode is a driven, damped oscillator with the complex QNM frequency, |χl(ω)| = Ωl²/|Ωl² − ω² − 2iωωI,l|, Ω² = ωR² + ωI². Modes add in quadrature with weights 1, 0.6, 0.4 (arbitrary; the paper finds l = m modes dominate when the binary's spin points along the equatorial light ring). Peak height ≈ Q, so faster spin = sharper, louder notes.
  • Offset: in this toy the loudest key is at √(ωR² − ωI²), a few % below Re ω. Santos et al. find the flux-at-infinity peak close to but not at Re ωQNM for a binary at the light ring, moving to lower frequencies as the binary moves out (their Fig. 7), and losing any clear link to the QNMs by r₀ ≈ 10M (Fig. 8). The toy reproduces only "close to but not at"; the binary is always at the light ring here.
  • Not modelled: the m-dependence and denser spectrum of Kerr modes (the paper notes Kerr resonances are sharper but harder to assign), overtones, horizon flux, the binary's orientation, and the geometric (non-resonant) flux peaks.
  • Game: a key counts as "found" for mode l when that mode dominates the response and |χl| ≥ 70 % of its peak. Two of three notes unlock the reveal.
  • Speed-up ×100 000 in time and frequency (vacuum GR scales exactly with mass, so this is the same as a black hole 10⁵ times lighter). Keyboard A2–C7 (110–2093 Hz) = 1.1–21 mHz real. Orbit of the pair: P = 2/fGW, Kepler for 10 + 10 M☉.
  • Sound: while a key is held, a soft triangle wave (the pair) plus a sine at the same pitch whose loudness follows the response. On release, a ringdown at the dominant mode's note with its own damping time τ = Q/(π f).
